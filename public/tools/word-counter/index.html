What the word counter shows

  • Words: any run of letters or numbers, including words with apostrophes or hyphens (don’t and well-known count as one word each).
  • Characters: every letter, number, space and punctuation mark. Many forms and social posts limit characters, not words.
  • Sentences and paragraphs: sentences end with a period, question mark or exclamation mark; paragraphs are separated by a blank line.
  • Reading time: based on an average silent reading speed of about 238 words per minute.
  • Speaking time: based on about 140 words per minute, a comfortable pace for a speech or presentation.

Set a word goal and the counter tells you how many words are left. The text and the goal are remembered on this device, so you can come back later.

How long is…?

Piece of writingTypical length
A paragraph100 to 200 words, 4 to 8 sentences
A short answer on a test50 to 150 words
A five-paragraph essay500 to 800 words
A college application essayoften 250 to 650 words (check the exact limit)
A one-page, double-spaced essayabout 250 to 300 words
A 5-minute speechabout 650 to 750 words
A blog postusually 600 to 1,500 words

Tips for hitting a word count

  • Too short? Add an example, a quotation or a counter-argument, rather than repeating yourself. Explain why a point matters.
  • Too long? Cut words like very, really and in order to. Remove sentences that only restate the one before.
  • Check the rules. Some teachers count titles and references, others don’t. When in doubt, ask.
  • Read it aloud. The speaking time helps you practice a presentation and stay within your slot.

Writing for a class?

When you write in DraftTrail, your teacher can see how your writing was built, step by step, which shows the work is your own. To check how easy your text is to read, try the readability checker.

Questions

Is my text private? Yes. Counting happens in your browser; your text is never uploaded.

Why might my word processor show a slightly different number? Programs treat dashes, numbers and links slightly differently. Differences of a word or two are normal.
