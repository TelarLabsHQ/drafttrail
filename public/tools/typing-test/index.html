How the typing test works

  1. Choose a time: 15 seconds, 30 seconds, 1 minute or 2 minutes.
  2. Choose the text: Common words (the most-used English words), Sentences (short paragraphs with capitals and punctuation), or My own text to practice a vocabulary list, a speech or a passage from class.
  3. Start typing. The timer starts with your first key. Correct letters turn dark, mistakes turn red, and you can fix them with Backspace.
  4. When time is up, you see your words per minute (WPM), accuracy and mistakes, your best score and your last tests on this device.

How WPM is calculated

A “word” in typing tests is any 5 characters, including spaces, so long and short words count fairly. WPM counts only the characters you typed correctly: correct characters ÷ 5 ÷ minutes. Raw WPM counts everything you typed, mistakes included. Accuracy is the share of keys you pressed correctly the first time, so fixed mistakes still count against it.

Example: in 1 minute you type 230 characters, and 220 of them are correct. Your WPM is 220 ÷ 5 = 44 WPM, and your raw WPM is 46.

What is a good typing speed?

  • Getting started: under 20 WPM. Everyone starts here.
  • Students: many type around 25 to 40 WPM.
  • Average adult: about 40 WPM.
  • Fast: 60 WPM and up. Professional typists often reach 70 to 90 WPM.

Accuracy matters more than speed. Aim for 95% or higher; then let speed grow.

Tips to type faster

  • Use the home row. Rest your left fingers on A S D F and your right on J K L ;. The bumps on F and J help you find them without looking.
  • Don’t look at the keyboard. It feels slower at first, but it’s the fastest way to improve.
  • Keep a steady rhythm instead of rushing and stopping.
  • Practice a little every day. Five minutes daily beats an hour once a week.
  • Sit up straight, with your wrists relaxed and the screen at eye level.

For teachers

  • Class challenge: run a 1-minute test, then tap Challenge a friend and share the link. Everyone gets the same text, so scores are fair to compare.
  • Warm-up before writing: a 30-second test gets fingers ready for an in-class essay in DraftTrail.
  • Vocabulary practice: paste this week’s words into My own text.

Questions

Can I take the test on a phone?

Yes. Tap the text and type with your phone’s keyboard. Scores are usually lower than on a real keyboard, so compare phone scores with phone scores.

Are my results saved?

Only in this browser on this device: your best score and your last 20 tests. Nothing is sent to us.
