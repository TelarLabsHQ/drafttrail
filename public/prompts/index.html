Writing prompts

Over 100 free writing prompts for elementary, middle and high school, sorted by type. Tap Write this on any prompt to open it in DraftTrail and start writing straight away.

NarrativeA narrative tells a story: real or imagined, with characters, a setting, a problem and how it turns out.PersuasivePersuasive writing tries to convince the reader to agree with you, or to do something, using reasons and evidence.OpinionOpinion writing shares what you think about a topic and explains why, with reasons that support your view.InformativeInformative or expository writing explains a topic, a process or an idea clearly, using facts rather than opinions.DescriptiveDescriptive writing paints a picture with words, so the reader can see, hear, smell, taste and feel what you describe.Reflective and journalReflective writing looks back on an experience to understand what it meant and what you learned. Journal prompts are a quick, low-pressure way to build the writing habit.

How to use these prompts

  • Students: pick a prompt, tap Write this, and write. DraftTrail records how your writing grows, so you can show your teacher it’s your own work.
  • Teachers: choose a prompt for the class and share the Write this link, or go to Set up a task to add a time limit and rules. Students hand in a file you can replay.
  • Warm-ups: journal and descriptive prompts make great 5 to 10 minute starters at the beginning of class.

Writing in the age of AI

Prompts like these are easy to paste into a chatbot. When students write in DraftTrail, teachers can replay the writing and see the typing, pauses, revisions and pastes. That makes the process visible, and it encourages students to do the thinking themselves. Read more in how to see the writing process in the age of AI.
