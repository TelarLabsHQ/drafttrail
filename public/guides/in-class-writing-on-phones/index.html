How to Run an In-Class Writing Task on Students’ Phones

Writing done in class shows what students can do on their own. With a live DraftTrail task, students can write on the phones they already have while you see, live, who leaves the writing page.

When to use it

  • Short answers and reflections at the end of a lesson.
  • Timed essays and exam questions that need a written answer.
  • A baseline sample early in the term, to compare with later take-home work.
  • Exit tickets: two or three sentences on what students learned today.

Set up the task

  1. Open the set-up page and type the assignment, for example “Explain how the water cycle affects our town during the rainy season.”
  2. Choose Live in class, with QuizPolice. It opens QuizPolice with a DraftTrail writing task.
  3. Choose how many exits are allowed before a student is locked, and add a timer if you want one.
  4. Show the code or QR on the board. Students join, type their name and section, and write.

On your dashboard you see, live, who is writing, who left the page and for how long, large pastes and word counts. When the time runs out, essays are handed in automatically. Essays are saved for up to 30 days and only you can see them.

Write prompts that suit phones

  • Keep the length realistic. Typing on a phone is slower than on a keyboard. 100 to 300 words in 20 to 30 minutes is a fair target for most students.
  • Show any reading on the board or on paper, so students don’t need to switch apps to see it. Switching away counts as leaving the page.
  • Ask for thinking, not recall. “Explain”, “compare”, “give an example from your own life” work better than “define”.
  • One task per session. Several short questions in one box are fine; number them in the prompt.

Prepare the class

  • Ask students to charge their phones and close other apps.
  • Tell them clearly what is recorded: leaving the writing page, large pastes and word counts. Not their screen, camera or other apps.
  • Explain your rules on voice typing and spelling suggestions before you start, so everyone follows the same rules.
  • Have a plan for students without phones: a school device, or paper.

During the task

  • Keep the dashboard open and walk around.
  • If a student is locked after a notification or a call, ask what happened and unlock them if you’re satisfied.
  • Give one student extra time from the dashboard if their phone had a problem.

After the task

  1. On the dashboard, choose Download all essays. You get one file with every student’s essay and its recording.
  2. Open the DraftTrail review page and drag the file in. Click a name to see the essay, the numbers and the replay.
  3. Look at flags as reasons to ask, not proof. On phones, predictive text and autocorrect can make typing look a little faster than on a keyboard.

Use it as a writing sample

Keep each student’s in-class piece. When a take-home essay later looks very different, you have a fair comparison, written in front of you, to talk about with the student. Read more in how to check if a student used AI, without an AI detector.

No reliable internet?

The live mode needs a connection to send updates to your dashboard. Without one, use the on their own mode: students write on the DraftTrail page, which works offline once opened, and hand in the .drafttrail file afterwards. You won’t see exits live, but every exit is still recorded in the file.
