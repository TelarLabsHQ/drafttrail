About DraftTrail

DraftTrail is a free writing-process recorder made by Telar Labs, a small independent studio.

AI tools can produce a finished essay in seconds, and AI detectors often get it wrong, sometimes accusing honest students. DraftTrail takes a different approach: instead of guessing from the final text, it keeps a record of how the text was written, which students can share to show their effort.

Why a record of the process

A finished essay can’t tell you how it was made. A history of the writing can: when it started, how it grew, what was rewritten, and what was pasted in. Teachers have always asked for drafts and outlines for this reason. DraftTrail does the same thing automatically, for every essay, without extra work for the student.

It helps honest students most. A student who is wrongly suspected can hand in the recording and show hours of real work, instead of trying to prove a negative.

Who it’s for

  • Teachers who assign essays, reflections or short answers and want a fair way to check the work.
  • Students who want proof that their writing is their own.
  • Schools that use Google Classroom or another learning system: the .drafttrail file is handed in like any other file.
  • Writing tutors who want to see where a student gets stuck, by watching how the draft develops.

How it’s built

  • Private by default. The writing and its recording are saved on the student’s device. Nothing is uploaded unless the teacher uses the live in-class mode with QuizPolice.
  • Checks that can be explained. Every flag is a simple rule in plain words, listed on How it works.
  • Useful for writing, too. Students get word counts, section headings, citations in APA, MLA and Chicago, and a reference list.
  • Works on any device with a modern browser, including phones, with no app to install.

Our promises

  • Free, no accounts.
  • Recordings stay on the student's device and in the file they choose to hand in.
  • No ads on the writing page or the review page.

Get started

  • Students: start writing.
  • Teachers: set up a task, read using DraftTrail with your class, or review files.
